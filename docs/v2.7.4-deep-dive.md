# antiRea v2.7.4 - deep dive

*One new advisory signal: a whole-cohort statistic from the topology line of the research harvest. It ships with its failed first form on the record.*

## Part 1 - What shipped

**Shipped:** `createHerdTightnessSignal`, an opt-in advisory detector for cohorts driven by one shared scheduler, ported from research candidate H7-11 after it passed its candidate-level lab round (cycle C148) - the first candidate-level verdict on that line, and the first build ordered from it. It is the companion of `createHerdPhaseSignal` (2.7.3), not a replacement: herd-phase confirms a fleet by the shared *offset* of its members' mean phases (R_cohort) and names the members of a confirmed cohort; herd-tightness reads the *shape of the whole cloud* of phase points and names nobody, ever. Nothing on the default path changes: no existing module imports the new one, and the full pre-existing suite (748 tests) passes unmodified.

**Not shipped:** nothing was excluded in this cycle - but the candidate's own first registered form was voided in the lab and replaced before it ever reached this repo (Part 3). The exclusion discipline is part of this release's record, not an exception to it.

## Part 2 - What the statistic is

Feed one homogeneous event stream per identity (session starts, room joins - the same feeding as herd-phase). An identity with at least 8 events becomes one point on the phase plane: its mean resultant vector on the 900 s scheduler grid, (R_i · cos φ̄_i, R_i · sin φ̄_i). The geometry is the detection idea:

- A fleet driven by **one shared scheduler** puts every member's point at nearly the same place - same grid offset, high resultant. The cloud is a tight knot.
- **Ordinary users** have low, varied resultants and personal angles: their points scatter in a disk near the origin.
- An **office-hours group behind one NAT** - the dangerous null, many real people who all look "scheduled" - still spreads: each person has their own rhythm, so the points spread even though the group shares an IP and a working day.

The binding statistic is the cloud's **H0 d0max**: the longest edge of the minimum spanning tree over the points, standardised per axis by a reference population (z = (x − median) / (1.4826 · MAD), constants from the reference cloud). d0max is the scale at which the cloud becomes one connected component - a direct measure of internal spread. Tight knot, small d0max; spread cloud, large d0max. Classification is a single frozen bar: tight iff d0max < bar.

## Part 3 - The H7-11 record (why it was eligible, and what failed first)

H7-11 came from the cross-discipline harvest (persistent homology / topological data analysis). Its registered mechanism was **H1 loops** - the idea that a herd's cloud would carry persistent one-dimensional holes. Step 0 measured exactly that and found **H1 counts of zero in every cell**: the proposed mechanism does not exist in this feature space. Under a weaker discipline the candidate would have died there or, worse, been quietly re-aimed. Instead the Step 1 registration did the honest thing in the open: it recorded the mechanism switch (the signal is **H0 connectivity** on the phase plane, not H1 loops), froze the new statistic (plane d0max) before running, and measured a clean distributional separation - bootstrap overlap **0.0** against both legitimate nulls (STEP1_OPEN, cycle C141).

The detector line then produced the round's most useful failure. The first candidate registration (v1) put a winsorize filter in front of the statistic, on the plausible-sounding premise that far-out points are junk. On the calibration draw the filter **emptied the entire herd cloud - 12 of 12 points** - because the shared herd sits far from the legitimate centre (offset 5.01-5.12 MADs) while being internally the tightest cloud measured. The run was declared **VOID** (a measured outcome, not a verdict), and a 7-draw sweep showed the required filter cap wandering from 2.5 to 5.5 with the reference population's scale noise: chasing the cap would never converge. The re-registration (v2) removed the filter outright - the statistic reads *internal* spread, so a point's distance from the centre is irrelevant to it - and froze the empty-cloud law away as moot, with the deletion and its reasons written into the registration.

v2 then passed at candidate level (cycle C148):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calibration (bootstrap extremes) | S_max 0.645374 | L_min 0.965498 | - | - |
| Test 1 (held out) | **0.277097** | 1.094551 | 1.722341 | 4.656567 |

Frozen bar: **0.805436** (the calibration midpoint). Every binding cell classified correctly on both held-out draws; the smallest relative margin was **35.9%** (test 1 LEGIT); no leave-one-out removal flips any classification on any draw; an independent verifier re-derived the verdict at 14/14 checks; two runs were byte-identical. HERD-INDEP is the control that defines the module's honesty boundary: a herd on per-identity grid offsets forms a *ring* on the phase plane and reads a large d0max - it is not flagged, and this module will never claim it.

## Part 4 - The shipped module's contract

- `observe({ identity, ts })` feeds the cohort under watch; `observeReference({ identity, ts })` feeds the standardisation baseline - the host's established, known-legitimate population. The lab standardised by its LEGIT draw; the module makes that population an explicit host input instead of pretending a universal scale exists.
- `evaluate()` returns `status: "scored"` with `tight`, the standardised `d0max`, the bar echoed, and the relative margin. Below 8 scored cohort identities it returns `"insufficient_population"` (the lab's cloud floor). **Without a scored reference population it returns `"uncalibrated"` with `tight: null`** - the raw d0max is reported for diagnostics, and the module classifies nothing, because no lab bar exists for the raw number.
- No winsorize, no filter, no cap on points - the v2 law, verbatim. Duplicate points cannot change d0max (zero-length edges join first; the lab's V5 property, pinned by a unit test).
- `enforcement: "none"` on every output. The result describes a cloud; there is deliberately no per-identity score, flag, or member list anywhere in the API.
- Pure helpers `phasePlanePoint` and `phaseCloudD0max` are exported for hosts (and tests) that build points from their own stored streams.
- Evaluate is O(n²) in scored identities (exact MST), so `maxIdentities` defaults to 4,096. The lab clouds were 9-16 points: this is a small-cohort instrument.

## Part 6 - What it does not claim

- **Not real-traffic performance.** Every number above is seeded simulation on one legitimate generator family (harness-v2). No production population has been measured with this statistic.
- **Not individual detection.** A tight reading is evidence about a cohort's shape. It cannot say which identities in the cloud are bots - a tight cloud could in principle contain legitimate identities swept into the evaluated population by the host's own scoping.
- **Not coverage of offset-randomising herds, other scheduler periods, or one-shot floods.** Rings read spread; only the 900 s grid is validated; identities under the 8-event floor are never scored.
- **Not safety for tight legitimate groups.** A legitimate population with its own shared machine rhythm (a device fleet polling in sync, a class starting a synced exercise) was not in the lab pools; if such a group is fed as a cohort it may read tight. That is exactly why the output is advisory, why it names nobody, and why hosts should scope cohorts deliberately and pair this signal with herd-phase (which *can* name members, under its own stricter confirmation law) before attaching any consequence.
