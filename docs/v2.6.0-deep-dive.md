# v2.6.0 deep dive

The full technical notes for the v2.6.0 release. The main README keeps a short, plain-language summary; everything below is the detailed version.

## New in v2.6.0: Action Budgets, Action Cohorts & Canary Actions - the feature-abuse layer

Version 2.5.0 metered the *data* side: how many distinct records an identity - or a coordinated cohort of identities - can receive. Version 2.6.0 adds the mirror layer for the *feature* side. The threat model is different in one crucial way: the attacker uses features **correctly**. Requests are valid, sessions are real, no protocol is violated. The abuse is aggregate - many cheap identities, each staying inside its own individual limits, jointly drain a shared resource: host compute and bandwidth on an expensive endpoint, a finite promo or trial pool, or simple fairness on one costly operation. A record budget cannot see this, because what is being consumed is the operation itself, not the records it returns. Both layers apply; neither replaces the other.

The layer ships three mechanisms - an action budget, an action cohort tracker, and canary actions - under the same discipline as the rest of the SDK: **no enforcement without a transaction, and no hard consequence on inferred evidence alone.**

## 1. Action budget (`createActionBudget`)

### Host-declared prices

The host declares a price table: abstract cost units per action type (export, heavy search, promo claim, and so on). The SDK never invents prices - what an operation costs is the host's business fact, not a library constant. A reserve request may also carry an explicit host-computed cost, which overrides the table entry for that call.

### Reserve / commit on the disclosure ledger's store contract

The hard problem is identical to the disclosure ledger's: a per-subject allowance plus a declared-cohort aggregate ceiling are only enforceable if check-and-reserve is atomic. The action budget therefore runs on the **same `LedgerStoreAdapter` contract** as `createDisclosureLedger` - the adapter and the bundled stores are imported, never forked. State is namespaced (`action-budget:<budgetId>`) so an action budget and a disclosure ledger can share one store without colliding.

- A store implementing `transactLedger` (CAS on the state version, covering allowances, reservations, ceilings, buckets and evidence accumulators in one transaction) runs `mode: "enforce"`.
- A get/put-only store runs `mode: "audit_only"`: every decision carries `enforced: false`, decisions are computed best-effort, and parallel reserves **can** jointly overspend. The module reports this instead of hiding it. In audit-only mode the only promise is the honesty of the ledger itself - what was recorded - not blocking.

Reservations follow the ledger's bounds, renamed for actions: reserve -> commit / release, TTL with lazy expiry inside the transaction (60 s by default), at most 2 outstanding reservations and 500 reserved cost per subject by default, an optional cohort-wide outstanding cap, idempotency keys, and a separate host-authorized long-reservation path (15-minute TTL) for slow operations. Bucket tokens spent by a reservation are **not** refunded on release - pacing is a cost, not a deposit.

### Declared cohort ceilings, armed at declaration

A host-declared action cohort's ceiling is **armed at declaration** - the 2.5.0 declared-ceiling law applied to actions. A member's reserves count against the cohort's per-window ceiling from the first action; no flag, crossing or warm-up is needed. A non-member reserving under the cohort id is refused `unregistered_subject`, an undeclared cohort id is refused `unknown_cohort` - both fail closed. Re-declaring a cohort updates membership and ceiling but does **not** reset its window accounting: re-declaring must not launder cost already counted.

### Pacing buckets

Two token buckets pace the flow: a global bucket over all reserves on the budget, and optional per-cohort pacing entries applied through `setCohortPacing`. A pacing refusal is a `rate_limited` result with a `retryAfterMs` - a rate, never a permanent denial, and it never touches subjects not listed in the pacing entry.

## 2. Action cohort tracker (`createActionCohortTracker`)

The budget meters what ONE identity may spend. The tracker watches the dimension a per-identity budget cannot see: what a GROUP of identities' actions cover, in aggregate. It is the herd detector's two grouping mechanisms transposed from record space to action target space - an action's target is the exported slice id, the corpus slice a search range covers, the promo id, or, generically, the endpoint id.

### Complement grouping

Identities whose target sets over the same action class are near-disjoint, with a large union, are grouped: disjoint slices of one export space, disjoint ranges of one corpus. Grouping is **leader-based**, as in herd v2: an identity's cluster is compared against other clusters' *aggregates* (one target refcount map per cluster), never against every other identity, and there is no per-pair state. An identity joins a cluster only if, after adding its targets, the cluster's overlap ratio (`1 - distinctTargets / totalTargetInstances`) stays at or below the link ceiling (0.10 by default). Only identities active inside the trailing window (15 minutes by default) count.

The **dilution gate** is the shape definition: a complement flag requires that at least **60%** of the group's members are individually at or below their subject allowance in the trailing window. The group is dangerous precisely because no member is; a group whose members are individually over their allowances (a small heavy team) does not complement-flag - per-subject budgets govern it.

### Shared-target grouping

Identities converging on the SAME `targetKey` - one promo, one endpoint - are the farming shape, where target sets are identical rather than complementary. Complement grouping correctly refuses to merge them (their overlap is maximal), so this mechanism counts distinct subjects and aggregate cost per target instead. Shared-target flags do **not** require the dilution gate: a promo pool is drained by claims that are each individually lawful.

### Materiality and friction targeting

Materiality is 1/12 of the flag floor per dimension, rounded up, minimum 1 - the herd law's derivation. How it is *applied* is mechanism-dependent (Fix B, section 5 below):

- **Complement:** a member's materiality is its **unique target contribution** - targets nobody else in the group holds. Raw cost alone never qualifies a complement member.
- **Shared-target:** a member's materiality is its **cost**, because its contribution is volume on the one shared target.

For supplementary re-announcement, materiality stays cost-or-unique, as on the data side. A flagged group re-announces itself (supplementary snapshot, coalesced to at most one per 5 s per group) when a member who was not yet announced with material contribution - a late joiner, or a pending partial contributor - crosses materiality. A member announced at exactly zero contribution is settled-inactive and never re-triggers on its own.

`actionFrictionTargets()` keeps only material members **minus protected members**: bystanders inside a flagged group get zero friction, as on the data side. Protected subjects accrue full telemetry and are listed under `protectedMembers`.

### IP is never a grouping key

IP is recorded on events but is **never** a grouping key. Identities sharing only an IP, with unrelated actions and targets, never group and never flag - a unit-tested invariant, as on the data side.

### The tracker never blocks

Like the herd detector, the tracker never blocks, bans or alters a response. It emits `onFlag` snapshots; the host decides the consequence, for inferred cohorts through the tiering clamp (section 4).

## 3. Canary actions (`createCanaryActionRegistry`)

The host registers target keys no legitimate UI ever emits: an export slice that is never linked, a promo id that is never shown. A touch is **direct per-subject evidence**: the tracker records a taint for the touching subject only.

- Taints never splash onto other subjects.
- A protected subject's own canary hit still taints that subject - a declaration is not a laundering lane.
- Attribution comes from the authenticated subject on the request, so a canary target cannot be used to frame another subject: the touch is recorded against whoever touched it.
- While a taint is live (the tracker's activity window), the tainted subject appears in the `taintedMembers` of any snapshot flagging on the same action class when it is a member of the group **or** its targets intersect the group's targets. Rotation into a fresh identity therefore stops resetting the clock at the first canary touch: the fresh identity that reaches for the same surface links itself to the flagged group through the taint.

## 4. The consequence law - the same tiering law as the data side

Consequences for the feature side follow the data side's law exactly, transposed:

- **Inferred cohorts** (tracker flags) cap at: **observe + step-up friction + a soft budget step-down with a floor**. The floor is *derived*, not chosen: `derivedSoftFloorCost` is the maximum single-action price in the host's table - the direct transpose of `SOFT_BUDGET_FLOOR_RECORDS` (itself derived from the disclosure ledger's per-subject reservation bound). A stepped-down subject always keeps exactly **one maximal legitimate action per window** - the price of the most expensive single action. Friction, never strangulation.
- **Hard locks** (a step-down to 0, a hard ceiling) require a **host-declared cohort or a ceiling-tier calibration manifest** - the host's own authorization decision, never inference alone. `clampActionStepDown()` delegates the tier decision to `clampEnforcement()` and applies the derived floor for inferred cohorts; a demand to cut an inferred cohort to zero comes back clamped to the floor, refused in code even when configuration demands it.

## 5. The fix round - three design fixes, no constant changed

Round 1 of the feature classic (section 6) failed its completion line and one canary sub-check. The fix round changed **design, not constants** - every shipped default below is the round-1 value.

- **Fix A - attempt-time evaluation (`evaluateAction`).** A pure query on the tracker: records nothing, mutates no state, fires no `onFlag`, records no taint, so a host may call it *before* serving an action and repeat it freely. It reports, per complement cluster of the event's action, whether the event would join that cluster, whether that cluster is already flagged, and whether the event's target is already known to the cluster (`targetKnown`). The host-side consequence: a joiner heading into an already-flagged cluster toward a **new** target is enrolled **at the attempt** - before it generates the member observations that flag-time enrollment used to wait for.
- **Fix B - mechanism-dependent materiality.** Described in section 2: complement friction targeting counts unique target contribution only; shared-target stays cost-based. Round 1's canary failure was the direct motivation: raw-cost materiality counted one export of cost 10 against a floor of 9, and a bystander was enrolled and overridden on the strength of a target others also held.
- **Fix C - pacing buckets keep spent state across membership changes.** Re-applying cohort pacing for the same scope updates membership and bucket parameters but **preserves the bucket's tokens and refill clock**. Before the fix, a membership change could hand a cohort that had already drained its burst a fresh one; after it, growing the subject list never resets pacing.

### Classification of the two fix-round FAIL lines

- **F1 flag union (14/24 vs the line of 16) - measurement-script vs architecture mismatch.** Fix A enrolls a joiner at the *attempt*, before it generates member observations. The flag-union counter counts observations inside flagged groups, so attempt-time enrollment - the fix working as designed - mechanically lowers what that counter can reach. Friction coverage measured the direct way, by enrollment, was **24/24**. The line is recorded as FAIL because it was pre-registered as written; the classification explains the miss, it does not erase it.
- **F2c in-window completion - the mathematical edge of the shipped pacing constants.** The harness pacing bucket refills at 0.1 tokens/s against an export price of 10: after the burst, the whole cohort refills at roughly **1 export per 100 s**. At that published rate, 40 operations cannot complete inside a 15-minute window once friction engages. Crucially, nothing was ever hard-denied: 0 hard denials across 3,205 attempts - work flows at the capped rate rather than stopping. This is a property of the constants the host chose for the round, stated here so a host choosing the same constants can predict the same arithmetic. The separate 75-minute confirmation run has since finished: the same team completed the same job **40/40 in 47 minutes 40 seconds** (2,860,482 ms; 7,222 attempts; 3,322 x 401, 480 x `subject_budget_exceeded`, 3,380 x `rate_limited`; 0 hard denials; measured serve rate steady at 6 exports per 10 minutes, matching the arithmetic above). The in-window FAIL above therefore stands as a window artifact, and the eventual-completion property the cell was designed to probe is confirmed at the window the constants require.

Read against the F1 baseline - 80/80 operations in 241 ms with 0 interrupts, unprotected - the protected side turned the same class of work into 220 attempts and 108 interrupts without completing it inside the driver's cap, with every participating identity enrolled. That is the layer's economic claim and its whole claim: abuse gets slower, more visible and more expensive per unit of result. It is not a claim that the work became impossible.

## 9. What this layer does not cover

- **The classic drivers are scripted.** No REA-driven or LLM-driven attacker was run against the feature side in this round. The numbers prove mechanics against scripted shapes, not detection rates against adaptive agents.
- **The bundled store is in-memory and single-process.** Enforcement exactness is proven for that wiring. A distributed store has not been proven: a host running multiple processes must supply a store implementing the same CAS contract, and until it does, the audit-only honesty rules apply.
- **S2 (heavy search) is wired and priced, but has no attack cell of its own.** The action budget prices search actions and the tracker observes them; no classic cell attacked the search surface specifically, so no cell result is claimed for it.
- **S3 (signup + promo farming) is covered at unit-test and wiring level only.** The shared-target mechanism and the canary registry are unit-tested and wired; there is no dedicated S3 classic cell, so no classic result is claimed for it.
- **Taint expiry and the shared-target supplementary path are coded but have no dedicated unit test.** Taints live for the tracker's activity window by construction, and shared-target groups implement the same supplementary re-announcement discipline as complement groups; neither behavior has a test of its own in this release, and both are stated here rather than silently assumed covered.
- **The audit-only race is a design boundary, not a bug.** Over a get/put store, the layer promises the honesty of the ledger - decisions marked `enforced: false`, overspend visible in the record - and explicitly does **not** promise blocking under concurrency.
- **Business-logic correctness is out of scope.** Whether a price is computed correctly, an inventory decrement is race-safe, an object belongs to the requester (IDOR / authorization), input is injection-safe, or authentication logic is sound are host code defects. A metering layer cannot detect or fix them, and nothing in this release advertises otherwise.

Suite for this release: **298 tests - 296 pass, 0 fail, 2 skipped** (pre-existing RSA-PSS vectors).
