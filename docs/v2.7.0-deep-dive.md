## Part 1 - The mathematical core: anytime-valid evidence

### 1.1 What was wrong with the old basis

The mixture GLR-CUSUM tripwire shipped through 2.6.0 accumulates
evidence well, but its threshold tables (`r6-calib-1`) were fit on
lab-simulated traces and are labeled uncalibrated. Every
false-alarm statement about it is therefore empirical: "in our
simulations, legitimate personas crossed at rate X." Nothing in
the construction bounds the false-alarm probability. 2.7.0 adds an
evidence engine whose bound comes from the construction itself.

### 1.2 Construction (`src/tier2-active/evalue-tripwire.ts`)

Per cohort, per observe-step t, the engine computes a bounded
extraction-intensity score from the cohort's exact served-record
union:

    x_t = min(1, novel_t / SCALE),  SCALE = 50 by default

where novel_t is the number of records in the step that are new to
the cohort's union. One `observe()` call is one step; the host
chooses the step granularity (this choice is load-bearing - see
1.5).

The null hypothesis is a single interpretable assumption:

    (A1)  There is a known mu0 in (0,1) with
          E[x_t | F_{t-1}] <= mu0 for all t, under legitimate use.

Betting e-value with a predictable stake lambda_t:

    e_t = 1 + lambda_t * (x_t - mu0),   K_t = prod_{s<=t} e_s

The stake blends a fixed design alternative (mu1* = min(1, 2*mu0),
Bernoulli log-optimal stake, halved as a floor) with a predictable
plug-in estimate of the alternative mean from past steps only
(shrinkage kappa = 20), clipped to [lambda_floor, 0.5/mu0] so every
e_t >= 0.5 and log-steps are bounded. Capital accumulates in log
space. The API accepts no stake input: a non-predictable stake is
impossible by shape (unit-tested).

**Guarantee (product mode).** Under (A1), E[e_t | F_{t-1}] <= 1
for every predictable stake sequence, so K_t is a nonnegative
supermartingale with K_0 = 1, and by Ville's inequality

    P_H0( sup_t K_t >= 1/alpha ) <= alpha.

Flagging at the first crossing of 1/alpha therefore has false-flag
probability <= alpha **at any stopping time** - continuous peeking
and data-dependent stopping are inside the guarantee. Tuning the
stake can only change power, never validity.

**What is claimed, exactly:** the application of anytime-valid
e-value accounting to cohort extraction detection, with this
guarantee structure, integrated with the tiering law. The base
mathematics is established (Ville 1939; Shafer & Vovk; Ramdas,
Grunwald, Vovk & Shafer 2023; e-detectors: Shin & Ramdas 2023;
predictable plug-in betting: Orabona & Jun 2023) and is not
claimed as new.

### 1.3 Merging cohorts (the rule, and why it is the only one)

Cohorts merge when grouping links identities. Capitals combine by
**weighted average** (weights = steps observed), constituents
frozen at merge; post-merge e-values multiply the averaged
capital. Product and max combinations are forbidden.

Why the average preserves the guarantee: at the merge time tau,
each frozen constituent is a constant with respect to the joined
filtration, and a constant process is trivially a supermartingale;
a predictable convex combination of nonnegative supermartingales
is a nonnegative supermartingale; and E[K_i,tau] <= 1 per
constituent (optional stopping), so the merged capital starts at
expectation <= 1. After the merge, validity reduces to the merged
cohort's own (A1) for its post-merge e-values - the same condition
as any cohort. The product rule fails because E[K_a * K_b] is not
bounded by 1 without independence; the max rule fails because
E[max] >= max of expectations runs the wrong way.

### 1.4 Simulation results (pre-registered, seeded)

Bars were registered before any run (PREREG-V270-MATH + Amendment
1, the latter driven by an external red-team round). Generators
mirror the calibration fitter's persona families (seed 0xC0FFEE
lineage; score sims seed 20271102). Full record:
study-side `v261/t1-results.md`, `t1-sim-raw.json`.

**Validity (M1) - PASS.** 10,000 legitimate runs, capital checked
at every step, horizon 2,000: **0 ever-crossings** at alpha 0.05
and 0.01 (one-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson upper 0.00030, bars 0.065 /
0.020). Least-favorable null (true mean exactly mu0, low-variance
shape): 13/4,000 = 0.325%.

**Validity under variance stress (probe M1b).** The least-
favorable null with MAXIMAL variance (x_t in {0,1}, P(1) = mu0 -
the shape that stresses Ville hardest) crossed at **4.175%**
(167/4,000, median crossing step 54) against nominal 5%. The
guarantee is near-tight at the true boundary; the low-variance
conservativeness above is a property of those nulls' variance,
not margin to spend.

**Detection (M2) - PARTIAL, as pre-defined.** Against the shipped
CUSUM's behavior on the same fitter traces (framing note below):
slicing traces - e-engine median **400** union records to cross
vs CUSUM 1,225, 16/16 both. ID-walk traces at per-record feeding -
e-engine **0/16**: a one-record step scores x = 0.02, an order of
magnitude under mu0, so the novel-union score at request
granularity is structurally blind to the detail channel. Score-
level runs (batched steps, E[x] = 0.26) cross 1,000/1,000 at
median 917.5 records. PARTIAL means, per the registered rule: the
engine ships as an **opt-in guarantee path**; the CUSUM remains
the default; no "replacement" wording anywhere.

Fairness note on the CUSUM comparison (both directions, as the
red team demanded): replaying the production CUSUM's shipped
defaults on the fitter's trace families gives an empirical null
crossing rate of 0.50 at nominal 1e-3 (NAT and onboarding families
cross 16/16) - while the classic cells of 2.5.0/2.6.0, at product
wiring granularity, show the same CUSUM holding its controls.
Both facts are about granularity and calibration fit, not about
one engine being "better": the trace harness feeds per-record
events; the classic harness feeds product-shaped requests. Step
granularity moves the legitimate and attack means of BOTH
engines, which is why 2.7.0 states a granularity contract (1.5)
instead of a winner.

**Misspecification price (M3, no bar - the number is the point).**
True legitimate mean at mu0 + 0.05: **1,500/1,500 cross** (median
86 steps). At mu0 + 0.10: 1,500/1,500 (median 42). The bulk-import
legitimate persona (E[x] = 0.36 > mu0): 1,000/1,000, median 26
steps. (A1) is not a soft assumption: a cohort class whose
legitimate sustained intensity exceeds the bound WILL be flagged,
with probability near 1 over long horizons. Section 1.7 exists
because of this table.

**Honesty floor.** An attacker sustained just below mu0
(E[x] = 0.18) crossed 0/2,000 - inside H0 by construction. The
engine is blind below the bound by design; that blindness is the
mimicry floor of section 1.9 restated in score space.

**Multiplicity (stated, not solved).** Ville is per-cohort.
Across N legitimate cohorts the expected number of lifetime false
flags is ~N*alpha, and P(at least one) ~ 1 - (1-alpha)^N: at
alpha = 0.05 and N = 100, ~5 expected false flags and P >= 99%.
Hosts must budget alpha across their cohort population.
Fleet-level budgeting (online FDR / alpha-investing) is the top
ranked next-wave candidate.

### 1.5 The step-granularity contract

mu0 is meaningless without a stated step definition. The same
traces fed in 60-second windows make the e-engine catch the
ID-walk (16/16, median union 331.5) - and also cross the
legitimate heavy-reader, NAT and onboarding traces 16/16, because
their per-minute novel-union mean exceeds mu0 * SCALE: at that
granularity (A1) is false for them. The contract shipped in the
module header: one observe() call is one step; set mu0 for the
granularity you actually feed; batching is part of the security
configuration, not a wiring detail.

### 1.6 The Shiryaev-Roberts mode (S1) - the capital-debt fix

A plain product process has no innocence floor: 1,000 quiet steps
at e ~ 0.9 drive log-capital to roughly -105, and an attack that
begins afterwards must first repay that debt. Measured on the
registered quiet-then-attack stream: product mode median **774
attack steps** to cross, vs 17 on a fresh cohort (~45x).

The module therefore also ships an SR e-detector mode
(`detector: "sr"`): M_0 = 0, M_n = (1 + M_{n-1}) * e_n over the
same e-values (equivalently the sum over all restart points of
the segment products), tracked in log space. Its guarantee shape
is DIFFERENT and is never conflated with Ville: under (A1),
E_0[M_n] <= n and the expected time to a false alarm satisfies
E_0[tau_B] >= B - **average-run-length control**, with B
(`srThreshold`, default 1,000) as the ARL knob.

Measured (PREREG-S1S3 bars D1-D3, all PASS): quiet-then-attack
median **14 attack steps** (bar <= 155; ~55x better than product
mode), 500/500 crossed; fresh slicing median **356 records**
(product mode 379 on identical runs; bar <= 1.25x); null
total-time-on-test **513 steps per crossing at B = 100** and
**5,376 at B = 1,000** (bars >= B/2), with all crossings
concentrated in the least-favorable family - whose own ARL sits
near the boundary (102.7 at B = 100), i.e. the SR bound is close
to tight exactly where the product bound was conservative.
Caveat carried in the header: after a cohort merge the SR
statistic is the step-weighted average of constituents, and the
ARL reading there is approximate.

Which mode should a host run? Product mode when the flag feeds a
consequence that needs the strongest false-flag statement and the
cohorts are continuously active; SR mode when cohorts have long
quiet histories or the threat model is burst-after-dormancy. The
CUSUM remains the default calibrated path; both e-modes are
opt-in evidence sources feeding the same tiering law.

### 1.7 Setting mu0 - and `calibrateMu0` (S3)

mu0 must bound the legitimate sustained intensity of every cohort
class the engine is pointed at, at the host's step granularity.
Procedure: (1) pick the step definition (what one observe() call
carries); (2) measure per-step scores over the host's legitimate
traffic per cohort class (the longitudinal farm exists to collect
exactly this); (3) set mu0 per class from the measured
distribution with margin - or use the shipped helper; (4) give
classes that legitimately run hot (bulk import, compliance
crawls) their own bound or the declared/protected lanes, which
the tiering law already keeps outside inferred consequences.

`calibrateMu0(scores, { delta })` implements step (3) as a
split-conformal bound: it returns the conformal upper order
statistic q of the calibration scores at level 1 - delta and
mu0 = min(1, q + delta). Derivation: for x in [0,1],
E[x] <= q + delta whenever P(x <= q) >= 1 - delta, and the
conformal order statistic gives that coverage finitely-sampled
and distribution-free under exchangeability of the calibration
scores. Measured (bars C1-C3): pooled calibration (n = 320,000)
gave q = 0.18, mu0 = 0.23 at delta 0.05 (held-out exceedance
4.23%, CP upper 4.27% <= 6%) and q = 0.22, mu0 = 0.23 at delta
0.01 (CP upper 0.15% <= 2%); held-out per-family means all
<= 0.23 (ordinary 0.080, heavy 0.140, onboarding 0.065,
NAT-aggregate 0.121); the engine at alpha 0.05 with the helper's
mu0 crossed 0/200 held-out runs in every family. Stated limits:
the conformal guarantee is MARGINAL and pooled - per-family
exceedance is not covered by a pooled calibration (NAT-aggregate
ran 11.6% step-exceedance at delta 0.05 while its MEAN stayed
well under mu0), so hosts with distinct traffic families should
calibrate per family; and calibrating a mean bound does not
discharge the conditional form of (A1) for adversarially-timed
legitimate bursts - the M3 table remains the price list.

### 1.8 Evidence accounting across detectors (doctrine, S2)

antiRea now runs several evidence consumers over one stream
(CUSUM, e-process/SR, herd detector, reviewer hook). The wave
adopts differential privacy's composition discipline as the
doctrine for that stack: evidence, like privacy loss, composes -
a host running k detectors is spending from one budget of
false-alarm probability and one pool of observations, and the
same observation must never be counted twice as if independent
(the rule Danny set for the advisory reviewer hook in 2.4.0 is
the local instance). Filters and odometers (harvest: Rogers et
al.; Whitehouse et al.) are the operational shapes: an odometer
reports accumulated spend, a filter halts at budget. A combined-
detector wrapper implementing this is a next-wave candidate; in
2.7.0 it is documentation, not code.

### 1.9 The boundary theorem (T4), stated properly

**Theorem (mimicry bound).** Let a defender's decision rule see
only request transcripts. Let P_L, P_A be the transcript
distributions induced by legitimate users and by an attacker.
For any rule whose block rate on legitimate traffic is <= alpha
and whose pass rate on the attacker is >= beta:

    |beta - alpha| <= TV(P_A, P_L).

**Corollary (leakage floor).** An attacker who can sample
transcripts from the fastest permitted legitimate workflow
achieves extraction at that workflow's rate with pass probability
at least 1 - alpha - (sampling error); no metering layer
observing the same transcripts can do better.

Assumptions, complete: (i) the defender observes nothing beyond
transcripts (no out-of-band identity cost, no client attestation);
(ii) mimicry is available to the attacker at some cost;
(iii) **the transcript model covers the channels through which
extraction can occur** - an unmetered surface is not a
counterexample to the bound, it is outside its scope (extraction
there is not bounded at all, it is simply unobserved).
Interactions: T1 does not move this bound (it controls false
alarms under the null; the bound governs the mimicry floor); an
information-weighted budget (T2) reparameterizes the leakage
axis, the bound's shape is unchanged. The theorem's operational
content is unchanged from what has driven the design all along:
the only levers that move the floor are identity cost and
entitlement scope - both host decisions. That is why the ID-walk
residual (section 2.6) remains a host-decision gate.

### 1.10 Parked math candidates

- **T2, information budget.** Formalized in the research doc;
  parked because the shipped union-count is already the
  degenerate information measure for distinct-item coverage, and
  a real MI estimator needs a dataset model the SDK deliberately
  does not hold. First honest step (host-tagged weighted union)
  is a ranked next-wave candidate.
- **T3, game-theoretic pricing.** The minimax derivation yields
  the shipped pacing SHAPE; its constants return through the
  assumptions. Analysis kept, build killed in synthesis.
- **Fleet FDR budgeting** (online alpha-investing over cohorts) -
  top-ranked next-wave candidate; see 1.4 multiplicity.

## Part 2 - The hardening track

### 2.1 L1 - declared-ceiling race: CLOSED

The 2.5.0 classic cell proved the declared ceiling exact (600)
in sequential wiring; LIMITATIONS carried the parallel case as
UNMEASURED. Cell (40 concurrent workers, ceiling 600, 18
identities, 5-25 ms serve latency): the check-then-serve path
overshot to union **639** (1-record shape) and **1,575**
(25-record shape). The fix is reservation-based enforcement on
the tripwire itself: `reserveServe` (synchronous check + tentative
hold in one run-to-completion step), `commitServe`, `releaseServe`,
with `ceilingReservation.ttlMs` (default 60,000). Re-run: **exactly
600, zero overshoot, both shapes**, 40/40 workers refused at the
cap; declared-protected control 1,200/1,200, zero interference.
18 unit tests cover the contract an external red team forced into
the open: a commit arriving after its hold expired is refused
`reservation_expired` and can never resurrect freed capacity
(settlement tombstones); the TTL must exceed the host's worst-case
serve latency, and serving outside a live reservation is outside
the cap's promise - both stated in the module header. Scope
honesty: this closes the race for hosts whose serves run through
ONE SDK process; cross-process exactness is the ledger's job
(2.2).

### 2.2 L3 - distributed store: reference implementation + kit

`examples/sqlite-ledger-store/` ships a transactional store on
Node's built-in `node:sqlite` (WAL + BEGIN IMMEDIATE CAS over the
same `antirea:ledger:<id>` key the SDK reads) and a conformance
kit any host store can be graded with. Cross-process proof: 2
processes, 1 shared file, ceiling 100, 400 requested - combined
granted **exactly 100, overshoot 0**, verified over a third
connection, three repeat runs identical in total. The kit passes
the in-memory and sqlite stores 4/4 and FAILS a deliberately lying
get/put store with the named reason `DISHONEST_TRANSACTION_CLAIM`.
Honest caveats: exactness is proven, **fairness is not** (grant
splits across processes ran 0/100, 0/100, 100/0, 10/90 - a host
needing fairness must add its own scheduling), and node:sqlite
needs Node >= 22.5 while the SDK supports >= 20, which is why the
store lives in examples/, not src/.

### 2.3 L9 - Web Bot Auth conformance pack

16 new tests build RFC 9421 signature bases independently from
the RFC's example key material (no vendor blob copied) and run
them against `createWebBotAuthVerifier`: 3/3 valid vectors verify;
**12/12 invalid vectors rejected with named reasons, false-accept
count 0** - including a signature valid over a DIFFERENT message
than the one presented (the failure mode an independent Oct-2026
analysis found in Visa TAP's and Mastercard Agent Pay's published
examples), the exact TAP shape of a 60-minute validity window
against a tight configured maximum, nonce replay, duplicate
covered components, expires <= created. No verifier defect was
found; the pack now runs in `npm run check` permanently.

### 2.4 L10 - policy signaling pack

`createPolicySignals` (new module) generates the honest
declaration layer: robots.txt `Content-Signal` lines (Cloudflare,
Sep 2025) and `Content-Usage` lines (IETF aipref draft
vocabulary), a TDMRep `/.well-known/tdmrep.json` document (W3C CG
final report - a JSON ARRAY of rule objects; the module follows
the report, not the single-object shorthand), and an antiRea
policy JSON tying them together with `enforcement: "none"`. It
declares the HOST's choices (opting into training is fully
expressible), validates inputs with named errors, and claims
**format conformance only**: no major crawler is known to consume
Content-Signal today, so no deflection is claimed anywhere.

### 2.5 L4 - feature-side cells S2/S3, and the L4-F1 defect

New classic cells on the 2.6.0 feature harness (pre-registered
bars). **S2 heavy search** (16 identities tiling 40 corpus
slices): flag covered **10/16** (bar >= 10), completion **12/40 =
30%** (bar <= 40%), control bar **FAILED** - and the failure was
a real defect, not a measurement artifact (below). **S3 promo
farming** (12 identities): flag **12/12**, 32 cohort credits,
per-identity grants capped at 2-3 per 60 s window (bound 20),
86 post-flag interrupts, control's first claim clean - PASS all
bars.

**L4-F1 (implementation defect, fixed in this release).** The
action cohort tracker's complement merge scored overlap on the
aggregate, so a singleton cluster whose only target duplicated a
herd target was absorbed into the herd at ratio exactly 0.10.
Two measured consequences: the bystander control landed INSIDE
the flag snapshot, and the absorbed duplicate zeroed the herd
member's unique-target count - contribution-gated friction
targeting therefore skipped that attacker entirely (0
interrupts, all 3 of its operations completed). Fix (registered
amendment before code): a complement merge/join now requires the
smaller side to contribute at least one target NEW to the union
or to carry at least 2 instances - a zero-new-target singleton
carries no grouping evidence. Re-run with identical drivers and
bars: S2 (a) PASS 10/16, (b) PASS **25.0%**, (c) PASS - control
in no snapshot, the previously shielded attacker enrolled with
2 interrupts, **16/16** attackers enrolled; S3 unchanged PASS.
The shared-target mechanism was deliberately untouched.

  cannot yet consume farm traces; the adapter is scoped

- **L8 sweep.** (a) `onConsequenceEligibility` callback fires

  scoring bit-for-bit unchanged): at weight 0.15 a synthetic
  interleaved evader flags at exactly 60.0 where the default
  peaks at 57.3 - and the legitimate colleague control's peak
  rises to 58.1, close to threshold, which is why it stays
  opt-in. (c) Canary-echo verification accepts a previous-secret
  window during rotation, with the generation labeled on every
  result. (d) Action-budget override visibility across processes
  was measured and is now a documented contract, with
  `flushOverrides()` for explicit publication. Plus the two
  missing action-layer unit tests (canary taint expiry;
  shared-target supplementary announcements) - both behaviors
  were already correct; the gaps were in test coverage, and
  LIMITATIONS said so until now.

### 2.7 Canary placement rule (doctrine, S4)

From the synthesis: a canary artifact is valid only if it matches
NO legitimate workflow shape. The host's legitimate workflow
catalogue is the "self set" (immunology's negative selection,
transposed); canary placement is censored against it. This
formalizes the construction argument canaries already relied on
- targets no legitimate UI ever emits - into a placement rule
hosts can audit.

## Part 3 - Advisory coverage signals and the grace window

Parts 1-2 describe the 2026-11-02 development wave. Three modules
were built after it, from the HARVEST2 research queue, under the
same discipline: pre-registered kill criteria in the lab first,
a build decision only after a lab PASS, and hard conditions in
code - not in documentation - wherever a measurement showed a
way to hurt legitimate users.

### 3.1 Input-space coverage - C6 (`createInputSpaceCoverage`)

**Lab verdict: PASS (first candidate of the wave through the
gate).** The record-coverage machinery (CUSUM streams, herd
union) measures *how much* of the dataset an identity or cohort
takes. It is structurally blind to enumeration that stays inside
legitimate features at legitimate volume - the P10 shape: a herd
sweeping a search/filter feature's input space, each member
polite, the union of *records* unremarkable. C6 accumulates
coverage statistics over the input space itself (feature
arguments, resource ids), in two families (F: feature space,
R: resource space), scored with ties-inclusive conformal
accumulation at identity and cohort level.

Lab numbers (3 seeds, harness-v2 populations; bars
pre-registered): legitimate flag rate **1.26%** pooled VALID
against a 2.0% bar (F 1.05% / R 0.76%); sale-week **1.20%** -
accumulated coverage statistics are NOT poisoned by a legitimate
sale spike, the mechanism that killed C3; declared-bulk cohorts
calibrated on their own pool **0.50%** against a 1% bar. Teeth:
sweep **9/9** and random-probe **9/9**, flagged at the first
checkpoint (20 queries), where the baseline novel-CUSUM scored
**0/9** on both; the feature-dispersed herd was flagged
**72/72 members plus cohort 3/3** where the union-resource
control scored **0/3**. Evader price, measured: a slowed sweep
is still flagged at the first checkpoint (time factor ~10,000x);
a rotator confined to a 120-id subset (6% extraction ceiling)
is flagged at delay 356-500 because its distinct-rate falls
BELOW the legitimate floor; the diagnostic price curve flags at
~2.9x the subset size. On classic resource shapes the module is
honestly slower than the baseline: ID-walk/slicing cohorts flag,
but at 480-816 events against the baseline's 11-13 steps -
coverage-set shape is slow evidence.

**SDK recheck (the shipped module, same populations and
seeds):** VALID false-flag rate **1.15%** (F 0.78% / R 0.36%),
sale **1.08%**, bulk **0.50%**; sweep/randprobe/slow-sweep 9/9
at query 20; dispersed herd 72/72 + cohort 3/3 with the union
control blind 3/3; ID-walk resource-family delay 608-688 events.
Suite cost: p99 0.0018 ms (F) / 0.0027 ms (R) per checkpoint.

**Hard conditions in code** (each one is a lab measurement, not
a preference): the host must DECLARE its enumerable surface -
no declaration, the instance is disabled and scores nobody;
features outside the declaration are never scored. Declared
(bulk) cohorts are routed to their own calibration pool and
report `uncalibrated` until that pool is fitted - they never
fall back to the casual pool, because scoring bulk traffic
against the casual pool measured **100% false flags** in the
lab. Pool floors (50 streams / 30 cohorts) throw instead of
calibrating on too little. Output is an advisory signal
(`enforcement: "none"`); a test pins the API surface - no
blocking or decision function exists.

**C6-1 - resolved in the cleanup wave (4.6).** The
pre-release audit reproduced that declared-cohort routing
latched per identity: one query tagged with a declared
cohortId at a checkpoint moved the identity's whole stream
into the declared lane, including its earlier untagged
queries. The question turned out to be decidable at SDK
level after all - the lab design treats a declared cohort
as a separate calibration STREAM, which never required
static membership - so the case was confirmed and fixed:
routing is now a function of each event (a query carrying a
declared cohortId scores in that identity's bulk lane; any
other query scores in the casual lane), state is split per
(identity x feature x lane), checkpoints close per lane,
and the hard condition survives at per-query granularity -
bulk traffic is still never scored against the casual pool.
Post-fix, the shipped module's full lab recheck is
byte-identical to the pre-fix recheck (VALID 1.147% / sale
1.083% / bulk 0.50%; sweep and random-probe 9/9 at the
first checkpoint; herd 72/72 + cohort 3/3). Only mixed-tag
identities see any behavior difference; single-lane
identities are untouched.

### 3.2 Asset/referrer profile - C9 (`createAssetReferrerProfile`)

**Lab verdict: PASS as a permanently capped auxiliary
feature.** Browsers that scrape typically load ~0.3 images per
page and send an empty referrer ~98.4% of the time; legitimate
browsers in the calibration corpus load ~22 images per page
with an empty referrer ~5% of the time. C9 scores accumulated
per-identity and per-cohort means of those two channels at
fixed horizons (50 pages / 120 pooled pages) with one-sided
ties-inclusive conformal p-values, PLUS a separation gate that
must also pass: the flag requires the score beyond threshold
AND the identity's image mean at or below a gate AND its
empty-referrer share at or above a gate, both gates derived
from TRAIN extremes against the published bot profile.

The anatomy is stated in the lab results and matters more than
the headline: the FP=0 result is carried by the separation
gate. The pure conformal score alone flags 1.23% of legitimate
streams (exactly nominal at alpha 0.01); the gate blocks all
82. The margins are wide - legitimate minimum image mean 15.32
against a gate at ~2.2 (6.9x), legitimate maximum referrer
share 0.18 against a gate at 0.582. Headline lab numbers:
VALID **0/6,658**, sale **0/923**, cohort **0/150**; teeth
9/9 raw, 9/9 polite, 9/9 naive-revisit (the baseline catches
only 6/9 of the revisiting shape); the one-hit herd is caught
in cohort form 3/3, the shape that is per-identity invisible
by arithmetic. Cost p99 0.004-0.007 ms per scoring.

**The cap is part of the verdict.** An evader that fakes the
full profile (loads images, sends referrers) escapes C9 itself
0/9 - but the whole system still catches it 9/9 through the
other layers, and faking costs it **22.8x** the bandwidth of a
raw bot (1.03x a legitimate browser). C9 is therefore a
bandwidth tax on evasion, forbidden from promotion to a
standalone detector.

**Hard conditions in code:** stratification is load-bearing.
The host must declare which traffic classes are neutral
(API clients, declared automation); only the declared browser
stratum is ever scored or accumulated. Scored as browsers, API
clients flag **1,500/1,500 (100%)** in the lab; after declared
stratification, 0. No declaration: the module disables itself.

**Shipped tighter than the registered lab design (03/11).**
Two mechanisms were added after the lab PASS, at the owner's
request, and re-measured at lab scale with the shipped module
before release: (a) a gate safety margin - `gateSafetyMargin`
default 0.25 moves both gates away from the legitimate region
by ~4x the one observed train-to-valid extreme drift (5.7% on
the image mean, 0% on referrer share); post-margin gates sit at
image mean 1.655 / referrer share 0.686, still far from the bot
profile (0.3 / 0.984); (b) two-window persistence - scoring runs
in consecutive non-overlapping windows, a first violating
window yields `watch`, a flag requires **two consecutive**
violating windows, one clean window resets. Lab recheck with
the tightened module: VALID **0 flag, 0 watch / 6,658**, sale
0/923, cohort 0/150 - the lab's zeros intact, including at
watch level. The price, measured and printed: identity flags
move from page 50 to page **100**, cohort flags from 120 to
**240** pooled pages, and the one-window one-hit herd now
reaches only watch (3/3) instead of flag - a deliberate trade
of single-window shapes for a second independent confirmation.

**Honest limit:** FP=0 is a harness number. Real-world
legitimate subpopulations that sit exactly in the bot corner -
images disabled, text browsers, RSS readers, referrer
blockers, undeclared legitimate clients - are NOT generated by
the harness and therefore NOT measured. That subpopulation's
size is measurable only on real host logs; it is recorded in
LIMITATIONS as the module's standing condition.

### 3.3 Post-verification grace (`createVerificationGrace`)

Built 03/11 from a gap the owner named: before this module,
nothing stopped the system from re-challenging the same
legitimate user at every new crossing for as long as their
(legitimate) behavior pattern stayed hot. Grace is a
per-identity record at the host's consequence layer - the same
layer as the protected-cohort gate, deliberately NOT inside
any engine: engines keep scoring and flagging throughout;
only the step-up ENROLLMENT is gated.

Contract: the host calls `recordVerified` when an identity
passes a step-up challenge, and consults `guardStepUp` before
enrolling a repeat step-up. Inside the window (default **24
hours**, parameter 1 s - 30 days) a repeat step-up at ordinary
evidence collapses to `observe`. Grace voids the moment
evidence reaches the escalation line - **2x the flag
threshold** by default - and the void LATCHES: dipping back
below the line does not restore grace; only a fresh passed
step-up starts a new window. (Without the latch, an attacker
could burst-dip-burst around the line and the escalation
threshold would mean nothing.) All window arithmetic runs on
the monotonic interval clock (L-DEEP-2 discipline): a
wall-clock step neither stretches nor shrinks a window; wall
time is kept only as an audit timestamp. State is capped at
2,000 identities with eviction failing toward MORE friction
(a lost grace record), never less. Invalid evidence
(NaN/Infinity) yields a structured `invalid_evidence` result
that suppresses nothing and mutates nothing - the F-09 lesson
applied at birth.

The structural guarantee, pinned by a test that wires a real
record budget (ceiling 5) beside the module: an identity
inside grace and a control identity are both refused at
exactly the 6th record - **grace buys zero records**. The
module holds no reference to any budget, quota or ceiling
(one-way, like the reviewer channel), so it cannot lift one.
What is NOT claimed: the 24 h window and 2x factor are policy
parameters anchored to the codebase's existing daily rhythm
and to LLR arithmetic (doubling a boundary squares the
likelihood ratio - a decisive escalation, not edge noise);
neither is calibrated on real traffic, and no production
deployment has yet measured how often repeat step-up actually
fired before grace existed.

## Part 4 - The consolidation wave and the pre-release audit

The 2.7.0 development tree forked from a state BEFORE the
2.6.1-2.6.3 fix waves. The 2026-11-03 pre-release audit
(independent, per-finding verifier rebuttals, reproducible
harnesses) found the consequence plainly: **every confirmed
2.6.0 finding F-01..F-16 reproduced verbatim on the 2.7 tree**
- including the HIGH (F-01: idempotency replay across subjects
in both ledgers) and ten MEDIUMs - because none of the shipped
fixes were in it. Releasing from that tree would have
re-published already-fixed vulnerabilities. This part records
what the consolidation did; users already on 2.6.3 already
have every behavior in 4.1 - for them it is an internal merge,
not a change.

### 4.1 The ported fix state (F-01..F-16 + L-DEEP-2)

Ported by behavior plus tests, never by overwriting files -
the 2.7 modules carry new code on top of the same files.
F-01 idempotency fingerprints (subject + payload) with
`idempotency_conflict` refusal in both ledgers; F-02 atomic
quota enforcement via `recordIfBelow` with the
`atomicEnforcement` honesty flag for legacy stores; F-03
export worst-case reservation (2x estimated rows, released at
settle); F-04 CSV/streamed bodies priced (write-wrapped,
settled over the whole body, flat price by estimate or floor);
F-05 unpriced actions refused `unknown_action` by default;
F-06 ledger fail-closed error handling (`store_conflict`
actually returned); F-07 sparse ledger bitmap (an id of 10^8
no longer allocates ~16.7 MB); F-08 campaign-flag
pending-and-retry delivery with throw-safe predicates; F-09
`checkServe` input validation (`invalid_request`), self-healing
refill, bucket reset on `clearCohort`; F-10 CUSUM state capped
(`maxIdentities` 2,000, LRU, `stateSizes` in snapshots); F-11
epoch rotation resets score + contributions + pending state;
F-12..F-13 idempotency/subject lifecycle pruning; **F-14
(behavior change):** an export whose budget identity cannot be
resolved is charged to a shared `unknown:unresolved` bucket
instead of silently skipping the budget - the legacy skip is
the explicit opt-in `allowUnresolvedIdentity`, the same
fail-loud pattern the quota guard already used; **F-15
(behavior change):** record-budget id keys for ids longer than
1,024 characters are hashed from a bounded sample (head, tail,
full length) - hashing cost per id is constant instead of
~160-220 ms for a 2M-character id, at the price of a wider
(and already accepted) collision family for pathological ids;
F-16 structured refusal of malformed CUSUM events before any
state is touched. The L-DEEP-2 monotonic discipline (12 points)
was ported with them - in the audited tree only
verification-grace still had it (audit CLK-1); the reservation
in the ledger already used the two-tier rule, and 4.2 extends
it to the one place that was still missing.

**Retest:** every audit harness was re-run against the fixed
tree and met its pre-registered pass criterion (the audit's
Notes for Retest table): stranger replays refused while
identical replays still return `duplicate: true`; atomic
stores hold exactly 5/12 while legacy stores carry the false
flag; parallel exports settle at or below the ceiling; all
four body paths priced; 0/30 unpriced grants; corrupt stores
refuse instead of throwing; extreme ids bounded; flags
re-delivered after a throwing callback; the
10 -> NaN -> 10 -> 10 sequence healthy; state capped with
`stateSizes` reported; no instant re-cross at +90 days; a +5
minute wall step no longer refills a spent budget. The fix
wave's own tests were ported with it: the suite grew from 424
to 520 tests in this wave, and to 585 after the cleanup wave
(4.6).

### 4.2 CLK-2 - reservation TTL on the operational clock

**New finding in 2.7 code, MEDIUM, fixed before release.**
`reserveServe` computed hold expiry from the wall clock
alone: a +61 s wall step (TTL default 60 s, ~0 ms of real
time) expired a live hold, a second full hold was granted,
and the declared hard ceiling of 50 was exceeded to a union
of 100 - while the first host, which had done everything
right, had its commit refused `reservation_expired`. The
audit's rebuttal of the module's "TTL contract = host
breach" defense stands: that contract presumes REAL serve
time exceeding the TTL; here the sole cause of expiry was a
clock step, an event the host neither causes nor controls.

The fix applies the rule the owner already approved for the
ledger's reservations: a hold dies at **whichever comes
first** - its wall-clock expiry OR its monotonic elapsed TTL
(`createdMonoMs` recorded in memory, injectable `monoNow`).
Operational reasoning for choosing monotonic-anchored
expiry over either pure clock: pure wall expiry is exactly
the failure above (an NTP step or a VM time correction
manufactures or destroys capacity); pure monotonic expiry
cannot be reconstructed after a process restart and drifts
from the wall timestamps hosts reconcile against in logs and
manifests. Dying at the earlier of the two keeps the hold's
promise under both failure modes: no step can extend a hold
past its TTL (monotonic elapsed is step-proof), and no hold
outlives the wall deadline the host published. Retest: with
only a wall step, the second hold is refused
(`campaign_ceiling`), the union stays exactly 50, and the
first hold's commit succeeds; a genuinely long serve still
expires on real elapsed time.

### 4.3 EV-1 - e-value callbacks: async rejections are swallowed

**New finding in 2.7 code, MEDIUM, fixed before release.**
The e-value engine wrapped `onCross` / `onSrCross` in a
synchronous try/catch while its interface promises "Errors
are swallowed" and its header states host wiring must never
break request handling. An async callback that rejected
escaped as an **unhandled rejection** - on a default Node
process, a crash - at exactly the crossing moment.
The fix adopts the pattern the CUSUM's
`onConsequenceEligibility` already used in this same tree:
capture the callback's return value and attach a swallowing
`.catch()` to any thenable. Retest: the audit harness
captures **0** unhandled rejections with a crossing decision
still delivered.

### 4.4 EV-2 / EV-3 - e-value input hygiene and calibration composability

**Both LOW, both fixed before release.** EV-2: the
constructor's `!(scaleRecords >= 1)` check admitted
`Infinity` (and `shrinkKappa = Infinity` passed its `> 0`
check the same way), after which every score computed to 0
and the engine was permanently, silently blind - 200 maximal
steps, no crossing, no error. Both options now require
`Number.isFinite`, with a named `RangeError` at construction
(the constructor already checked `srThreshold` this way; the
two options were simply missed). EV-3: `calibrateMu0` could
return `mu0 = 1` - a value `createEvalueTripwire` itself
rejects - so the calibration helper's output was not always
usable by the engine it feeds. The helper now caps the
returned value below 1 and flags the result
`saturated: true` when the conformal bound saturates
(quantile + delta >= 1): the value is an acceptable engine
input but explicitly NOT a proven bound, which is the honest
description of a saturated calibration.

### 4.5 KIT-1 - the conformance kit runs as documented

The sqlite store's conformance kit, invoked exactly as its
README documents (factory path, no `--label`), printed the
usage message and exited 2: with `labelIdx = -1`, the
argument scan excluded index 0 - the factory path itself.
Fixed in the argument parser (the label value index applies
only when `--label` is present); verified live: sqlite and
in-memory fixtures PASS 4/4, the lying get/put fixture FAILs
with `DISHONEST_TRANSACTION_CLAIM`, `--label` still works,
and a genuinely argument-less invocation still prints usage
with exit 2.

### 4.6 The cleanup wave - closing the audit's remainder

After the consolidation, three buckets remained: C6-1 as
needs_validation, the low-severity leftovers F-17..F-24 of
the 2.6.0 audit, and that audit's four needs_validation
cases NV-1..NV-4. A final cleanup wave re-verified every
one of them on this tree: all thirteen reproduced, all
thirteen were fixed, and the suite grew from 520 to **585
tests** (+65 regression tests, each with fail-before /
pass-after evidence). No needs_validation case remains open
in the audit's scope.

**C6-1 - confirmed, fixed per-event.** The routing question
was decidable without host facts: the lab design defines a
declared cohort as its own calibration stream, not a static
membership, so the latch was a defect. Routing is now a
function of each event - a query carrying a declared
`cohortId` scores in the identity's bulk lane, any other
query in the casual lane; state splits per (identity x
feature x lane); checkpoints close per lane;
`evaluateCohort` aggregates the casual lane only; a
`cohortId` on a resource event no longer routes queries.
The hard condition (never score bulk traffic with the
casual pool) survives per query. The shipped module's lab
recheck after the fix is byte-identical to before: VALID
1.147%, sale 1.083%, bulk 0.50%, sweep/randprobe 9/9 at
delay 20, herd 72/72 + cohort 3/3 - no cell regressed.
Single-lane identities see no change.

**F-17..F-24 - all confirmed, all fixed.** F-17:
`evaluateAction` now judges only the slice of a group still
live inside the activity window (a pure read), so a dead
group's flag is never reported stale. F-18: the
action-cohort tracker prunes every map (`pruneAll`) on
observe and on read. F-19: `evidence-attribution` gains a
`maxPairs` option defaulting to **1,024** pairs with LRU
eviction - above the cap, an evicted pair's cross-credit is
forgotten and restarts from zero. F-20: host callbacks are
wrapped in the package's swallow-discipline at the
remaining bare sites (herd `recordIndex` / `getLimit`,
action-cohort `getAllowance`, flow-scorer `isExempt`) - a
throwing callback can no longer abort an observe or leave
half-mutated state, and a non-boolean truthy `isExempt`
result now counts as not exempt, matching the CUSUM
precedent. F-21: both herd modules validate the event
timestamp before touching any state - a non-finite `ts` or
|ts| beyond the Date range (8.64e15) is refused cleanly
(`{ recorded: false }`, no throw, no mutation, no poisoned
latch swallowing a later legitimate flag); the same
validation closes the action-cohort variant, where the bad
timestamp pruned live state instead of throwing. F-22: the
Structured Fields parser now rejects duplicate parameters
and duplicate dictionary keys per RFC 8941, surfacing as
`malformed_headers`. F-23: Cursor V2's authority key is
length-prefixed (`${subject.length}:${subject}` +
`${scope.length}:${scope}`), so NUL-shifted (subject,
scope) pairs can no longer share a counter. F-24: Canary
Maze eviction removes only expired entries; a map full of
live entries refuses the new IP with the cap response
instead of evicting a live victim and resetting its count.

**NV-1..NV-4 - all confirmed at SDK level, all fixed.**
NV-1: the tripwire validates cursor and dwell before they
reach the velocity stream - cursor fields must be finite
and dwell finite within [0, 3,600,000] ms per record; an
invalid cursor is treated as absent (no EWMA touch, no
increment, no throw). NV-2: all three token verifiers
(cursor, cursor-v2, canary-echo) reject tokens longer than
**8,192 characters** as `malformed` BEFORE any decode or
parse - the measured worst-case verify cost drops from
~3,322 ms (a 10 MB token) to ~0.003 ms per call. This is
the wave's largest behavior change: a correctly signed
token above the cap is refused, so a host minting unusually
long fields would reject its own tokens (typical valid
tokens are 140-190 characters; the caps are exported as
`MAX_CURSOR_TOKEN_LENGTH`, `MAX_CURSOR_V2_TOKEN_LENGTH`,
`MAX_ECHO_ID_LENGTH`). NV-3: the Web Bot Auth directory
path fails fast - `parseWebBotAuthDirectory` refuses
documents over **256 keys** or **1,048,576 characters**
(real directories carry 1-10 keys; over-cap input fails
closed as `key_resolution_failed` on the resolver path, or
as a TypeError at construction on the static `keys:` path),
and parsed directories are cached per verifier (LRU 8 by
content for strings, WeakMap by identity for objects),
cutting the README-documented resolver wiring from ~214 ms
per verify on a 10,000-key directory to the already-parsed
path (~0.2 ms) after the first parse. NV-4: the decoy
mirage's response write is fully wrapped - a response
object that throws synchronously, or returns a rejecting
promise, can no longer reject the host middleware or leak
an unhandled rejection; a healthy response behaves
byte-for-byte as before.
